How it works

Variance treats gains and losses the same, but nobody cries about gains. CVaR (Conditional Value at Risk) only looks at what hurts: the average loss of the worst days. At 95% confidence, it is the mean of the worst 5% of historical scenarios.

Minimizing CVaR looks like an impossible statistics problem, but Rockafellar and Uryasev proved it turns exactly into a linear program: each historical day is a scenario, each scenario adds one constraint, and the simplex finds the optimal corner. The weights that come out are the ones that would have best protected against the worst stretches of the last 5 years, within your limits.

The piece that makes it useful is the return target: without it, the mathematically correct answer is boring (100% cash, which almost never loses). By demanding a minimum expected return, the LP is forced to take risk, and the simplex finds the corner that does it with the least possible pain. It is the usual efficient frontier, just measuring risk on the side that matters.

The minimum variance portfolio is the classic reference (Markowitz without looking at returns): it minimizes total volatility and, since it asks for no return, it also hides in cash. Comparing the three shows the difference between "doesn't move" and "doesn't crash while chasing a target".

Scenarios: historical days from the last 5 years (resampled so the LP stays fast on mobile). Cash is a US T-bill ETF (BIL). None of this is investment advice.
